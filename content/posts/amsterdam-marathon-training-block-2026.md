The Amsterdam build ran for 23 weeks, from May 11 to race day on October 18. The training-block spreadsheet logs 2,171.07 km across the block, with a peak week of 128.27 km. Here is how the work progressed from sub-threshold base running to race day.

The weekly distance and time totals above are from the spreadsheet. Paces below are minutes per kilometre. Race paces are calculated from the result times in the sheet; activity paces are full-session averages from the FIT files, so they include warm-up, recoveries, and cool-down where those were recorded.

## 1. Sub-threshold base — weeks 1–13

The first phase built a consistent aerobic and workout rhythm. Most weeks paired easy running and a long run with two or three quality sessions: the sheet moves from efforts such as **3 × 10 minutes** and **5 × 6 minutes** toward **4 × 12 minutes**, **6 × 6 minutes**, and short **3-minute repetitions**. Long runs grew as high as 29.02 km in week 13.

Volume averaged 90.28 km per week, rising from 65.73 km in week 1 to 110.47 km in week 13. There were lighter weeks along the way, including travel and rest in week 4 and a reduced week 12.

**Activity snapshot:** the August 9 long run was 29.02 km in 2:26 at an average of about **5:01/km**.

## 2. Interval increase — weeks 14–18

The sessions shifted toward longer and more specific repetitions: **3 × 5 km**, **3 × 2 km**, **4 × 5 km**, and **8 × 1 km**, alongside easy running and long runs. The phase included a lighter travel week, then reached its volume high at 120.31 km in week 16.

Two results in this stretch mark the sharpening: a **17:50 parkrun** (about **3:34/km**) in week 14 and a **36:41 10 km** (about **3:40/km**) in week 17.

**Activity snapshot:** the August 25 **4 × 5 km** session covered 25.31 km in 1:55, averaging about **4:34/km** over the whole activity. That average is not the pace of the repetitions alone.

## 3. Race specific — weeks 19–21

The final full training phase focused on holding quality over longer distances. The schedule features **3 × 5 km**, **5 × 2 km**, **8 × 1 km**, and **5 × 5 km**, supported by medium and long runs. Weekly volume climbed to the block high of **128.27 km** in week 21.

The half marathon in week 20 was a **1:20:52** (about **3:50/km**). It was a strong marker of fitness heading into the taper.

## 4. Taper and race — weeks 22–23

The taper kept short, controlled work in the plan—such as **3 × 8 km**, **8 × 1 km**, and **5 km steady-state**—while reducing the amount of training before race day. Week 23 totals 92.83 km because it includes the marathon; excluding the 42.77 km race entry, the week's other recorded distance was 50.06 km.

The spreadsheet records an Amsterdam Marathon personal best of **2:49:34** (about **4:02/km**) and a chip time of **2:52** (about **4:05/km**).
